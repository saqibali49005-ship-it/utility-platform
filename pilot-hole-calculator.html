Find your drill-bit size

I don't know my screw size

Measure the screw's outside/major diameter with a ruler or caliper. If possible, also measure the threaded root diameter.

Understand the three hole types

Pilot hole: helps the threaded part of the screw enter the material with less splitting and driving resistance.
Clearance hole: lets the screw pass through the upper piece so the threads mainly engage the lower piece.
Countersink: creates a recess for a compatible flat/countersunk screw head.
Important: there is no universal pilot-hole size. Screw geometry and material matter. Use the result as a starting point and follow manufacturer instructions for critical work.

How to use the calculator

  1. Select the screw closest to your fastener.
  2. Select the material.
  3. Choose pilot, clearance or pilot + countersink.
  4. Use the metric and imperial result to select a practical bit.
  5. Test on scrap of the same material when the joint is important.

Frequently asked questions

What is a pilot hole?

A pilot hole is a smaller hole drilled before driving a screw. It helps guide the screw and can reduce splitting, wandering and excessive driving force.

Is a pilot hole the same as a clearance hole?

No. A pilot hole helps the screw's threads engage. A clearance hole is large enough for the screw to pass through the upper layer without threading into it.

Does material change the recommended size?

Yes. Hardwood, softwood, plywood and MDF behave differently, and screw geometry also matters.

Should I always use the exact calculated size?

Not necessarily. A close common bit can be more practical. For critical work, verify the fastener manufacturer's recommendation.
