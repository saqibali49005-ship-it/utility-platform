  2. Use the conduit-fill calculator to check whether the chosen conductor combination fits the selected raceway.
  3. Complete the other required checks, including ampacity, adjustment and correction factors, grounding and bonding, temperature, terminal ratings, protection and local code requirements.

Electrical planning workflow

Wire selection and raceway fill answer different questions. Voltage-drop calculations estimate how much voltage may be lost along a conductor run under the entered assumptions. Raceway-fill calculations compare the combined conductor area with the permitted interior area of a selected raceway. A result that passes one check does not automatically pass the other.

  1. Define the circuit: record the system voltage, load or current, conductor material, circuit type and one-way route length.
  2. Estimate voltage drop: use the wire-size calculator as an initial comparison, then confirm conductor resistance and operating conditions from the actual cable data.
  3. Check raceway space: enter every conductor in the conduit-fill calculator, including the equipment grounding conductor where applicable.
  4. Complete the design checks: verify ampacity, terminal temperature ratings, conductor insulation, derating, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, installation method and the code adopted by the authority having jurisdiction.

Key terms

  • Voltage drop: the reduction in voltage between the source and the load caused by circuit impedance.
  • One-way length: the physical route distance from source to load; the electrical calculation must account for the complete circuit path.
  • Conduit fill: the portion of the raceway's internal cross-sectional area occupied by conductors.
  • Ampacity: the current a conductor is permitted to carry under specified installation and temperature conditions.

Standards and further checking

For installations governed by the US National Electrical Code, consult the edition adopted by the local authority and the official NFPA 70 (National Electrical Code) information. Requirements and amendments vary by jurisdiction; do not assume that a web calculator establishes compliance.

Important: these tools are planning aids, not a certification of code compliance. Electrical requirements vary by jurisdiction, equipment, conductor construction and installation conditions. Follow applicable code, manufacturer instructions and qualified electrical guidance where required.
