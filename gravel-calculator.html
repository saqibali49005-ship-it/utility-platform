How the gravel math works

Volume

volume = area × installed depth

For a rectangle, area = length × width. For a circle, area = π × radius². For a triangle, area = base × height ÷ 2. Each section is converted to cubic feet/metres and then summed.

Weight

weight = volume × material density

In US units, a density in tons/yd³ converts cubic yards to US short tons. In metric, kg/m³ converts cubic metres to kilograms.

Allowance is applied after the measured volume is summed. Keeping the unrounded volume until the final order step avoids unnecessary rounding error.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate how much gravel I need?

Measure the surface area and the installed depth, calculate the volume, then use a supplier or material density to estimate weight. This calculator also lets you combine multiple areas and add an optional ordering allowance.

What density should I use?

Use the exact bulk density from your supplier or delivery paperwork when available. Presets here are planning estimates and can vary with stone type, source, moisture and compaction.

How much extra gravel should I order?

There is no single percentage that is correct for every site. Use the allowance field for spillage, uneven subgrade and similar losses, based on your project conditions or supplier advice.

Can I calculate an irregular project?

Yes. Add sections and split the project into simple shapes. The calculator sums the volumes before applying the common allowance.

Are the tons exact?

No. Your measured volume is determined by the dimensions and depth entered. Weight depends on the density assumption, so the final delivered weight may differ from the estimate.
